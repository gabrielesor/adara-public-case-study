# Compliance and Audit

Adara treats compliance as part of controlled execution, operational monitoring, and retained evidence. Those are related but distinct responsibilities: applicable controls can prevent an Adara-originated order from reaching an exchange, earlier warning thresholds can support preventive operational attention, and retained reports can document order- and portfolio-level compliance context.

This practical engineering model supports operational traceability and review. It is not a claim of regulatory certification, legal audit status, universal control effectiveness, or formal evidentiary properties.

## Scope

This page describes pre-trade enforcement for orders originated through Adara, the context available to applicable controls, public control categories, the distinction between enforced limits and early-warning thresholds, multi-channel notifications, order-level evidence, scheduled daily portfolio reporting, and reviewable compliance history.

The page uses “auditability” only in the practical sense that retained operational state, provenance, and compliance evidence support traceability and operational review. It does not expose exact rules, thresholds, formulas, identities, real orders or portfolios, provider configuration, notification recipients, security settings, or proprietary operational procedures. It does not assert compliance with a named regulatory framework.

## Compliance in the execution path

For orders originated through Adara, applicable compliance controls are evaluated before exchange submission. The same compliance layer applies to discretionary, human-directed orders and to orders generated by automated strategies inside the wider platform.

The controlled path begins with trading intent and available operating context. After applicable validation, compliance evaluates the order against the controls relevant to that activity. A failed applicable control stops the order before the exchange boundary. A successful evaluation permits processing to continue through any applicable confirmation and toward exchange submission.

This placement is important because compliance is not only a report produced after trading. It participates directly in determining whether an Adara-originated order can continue. The platform therefore connects control inputs to execution before an order is submitted while retaining evidence for the successful controlled path.

The scope of that statement is deliberate. Exchange activity that originated outside Adara may later be synchronized or observed, but its presence in the platform does not mean Adara applied pre-trade controls before the external submission. See [Order and Trading Lifecycle](06-order-and-trading-lifecycle.md) for the detailed provenance distinction.

## Compliance context

Compliance evaluation can use order and portfolio information at a normalized operational level. Relevant context may include normalized order value, account state, account exposure, aggregate portfolio exposure, portfolio concentration, and asset-classification context.

Different trading pairs and portfolio assets may express value through heterogeneous currencies or pairs. Normalization into a common reference value gives controls and reporting a consistent basis for comparison without requiring each rule or report to preserve external quotation conventions as its primary analytical unit.

The public case study documents the responsibility for preparing that context, not its formulas. It does not identify a target currency or token, intermediate conversion path, price-selection method, portfolio value, account balance, exposure value, or evaluation algorithm.

## Public control categories

The public compliance model can be described through categories of controlled concern rather than internal rule identifiers. Those categories include:

- **Single-trade size** — control context associated with the normalized value of an individual proposed trade.
- **Account exposure** — control context based on the exposure represented at account level.
- **Overall portfolio exposure** — control context based on exposure across the relevant portfolio state.
- **Portfolio or asset-category concentration** — control context associated with how exposure is distributed within the portfolio and its classification model.

These descriptions establish capability boundaries without defining the rule implementation. No numeric limit, threshold, percentage, grace period, formula, or internal identifier is published. The categories also do not imply that one public label maps to a single class, database row, module, or deployment component.

## Enforced limits and early-warning thresholds

Adara distinguishes conceptually between enforced compliance limits and earlier warning thresholds used for operational monitoring.

An enforced limit belongs to the applicable compliance evaluation. If that limit is breached for an Adara-originated order, the control fails and the order is prevented from being submitted to the exchange. The result changes whether the controlled execution path may continue.

An early-warning threshold serves a different purpose. It can identify a monitored condition before a formal limit is breached and trigger operational attention through notifications. Crossing that warning threshold is not, by itself, necessarily a formal compliance-rule violation. It indicates that the monitored state has reached an earlier notification boundary.

Keeping these concepts separate avoids describing every warning as a failed control. It also allows preventive monitoring to coexist with enforced pre-trade decisions without disclosing actual values, timing, escalation procedures, or governance arrangements.

## Multi-channel notifications

Warning and compliance events can trigger multi-channel notifications. At the public level, this means the platform can direct operational attention to monitored conditions through more than one notification channel.

The notification capability is described generically. This case study does not identify channels, addresses, recipients, distribution lists, configuration, stakeholder-specific escalation paths, or delivery guarantees. Notifications support awareness and review; they do not replace the enforced decision made by applicable pre-trade controls.

## Order-level compliance evidence

For an Adara-originated order that successfully passes applicable controls and proceeds through the controlled execution path, Adara can generate an order-level compliance PDF. The PDF documents the compliance evaluation associated with that order, is distributed by email, and is retained server-side.

This evidence belongs to the successfully controlled order path. The public description does not state that a rejected pre-trade attempt generates the same order-level PDF, while making no claim that failed attempts leave no internal trace. The distinction avoids extending the known report behavior beyond the authorized public facts.

The report itself is not published. Its internal layout, real order identifier, actual control values, account context, recipients, address list, and retention implementation remain outside the public scope.

## Daily portfolio-level compliance reporting

Adara supports scheduled daily portfolio-level compliance reporting. A daily portfolio compliance PDF represents compliance status at portfolio level, is distributed by email, and is retained server-side.

This report is distinct from the order-level PDF. The order view documents evaluation associated with an individual successfully controlled order, while the portfolio view records scheduled portfolio-level compliance status. The daily report is not described as an aggregation or sum of order-level PDFs.

The public case study does not state an exact execution time, identify recipients, reproduce report contents, disclose positions or exposure values, or claim that a report was produced on every historical day without exception. It describes the supported scheduled reporting responsibility.

## Operational traceability

Order provenance, retained lifecycle state, compliance evidence, and historical compliance views provide a reviewable operational history. Together, these records can support tracing how controlled activity was represented, what origin was retained, and what compliance evidence is associated with the successful order path.

Adara also supports current or recent compliance state and historical compliance review at a capability level. Orders can be reviewed in the context of applicable compliance controls, and retained portfolio reports provide a separate historical evidence view.

This is operational traceability, not a claim of storage immutability, cryptographic non-repudiation, formal legal evidence, formal audit assurance, or regulatory approval. The architecture does not specify retention guarantees, event sourcing, transaction boundaries, or a formal audit standard.

## Relationship to externally originated activity

Externally originated exchange activity may be synchronized, observed, and retained in Adara's operational history. Provenance preserves the fact that the activity originated outside the platform.

Such activity is not retroactively described as having passed through Adara's pre-trade compliance gate. It may appear in operational or historical views without carrying a claim that Adara authorized, validated, or compliance-checked it before it reached the exchange.

This separation matters for review: a coherent operational history can contain both controlled execution and observed external activity while retaining the difference in origin and control scope. Visibility is not rewritten as prior approval.

## Compliance flow

The [Compliance Flow Diagram](../diagrams/compliance-flow.md) presents pre-trade enforcement and portfolio monitoring/reporting as two related flows. Five details are central:

1. Compliance sits before exchange submission for orders originated through Adara.
2. A failed applicable control terminates the controlled path before the exchange boundary.
3. A successful result permits controlled execution to proceed and is associated with order-level evidence.
4. Early-warning monitoring is separate from enforcement and can generate multi-channel warnings before a formal breach.
5. Order-level and daily portfolio-level PDF reports are distinct evidence views with separate distribution and retention responsibilities.

The diagram is a public responsibility view. It does not define a detailed rules engine, exact transactional order, or legal compliance model.
